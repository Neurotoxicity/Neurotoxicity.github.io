Finished Reading: Sōbōtei Must Be Destroyed!
[17-11-2026]
For the last couple weeks I've been reading <i>Sōbōtei Kowasubeshi</i> (双亡亭壊すべし; lit. 'Sōbōtei Must Be Destroyed'), which is a manga by Kazuhiro Fujita that serialised between 2016 and 2021. I was kind of shocked to discover that it's a pretty recent manga, cause the artstyle really harks back to 90s manga in a really fun way. You don't really see drawings like that so much these days. Between this one and <i>Heavenly Delusion</i> (masakazu Ishiguro, 2018-ongoing), it feels like there's a certain slight revival of different old-school art styles happening, which is honestly super exciting.<br>
Manga audiences have kind of always favoured more detailed styles, and that tendency has driven the mainstream strains in the medium towards more and more complex visuals. As digital tools have gained more prevalence, shit has gotten crazier and crazier. Which has produced a lot of incredible art, obviously, but it's also moved the medium away from it's roots as something produced by pen-marks - some artists, like Boichi (artist of <i>Dr. Stone</i> and more recently <i>The Marshall King</i>), have attained such a high level of polish that no individual marks are discernable at all. <br>
Although these artists are undeniably skilled (more so than me, certainly) and their work is undeniably gorgeous (more so than mine, for sure), I still have a preference for illustrative styles that still show the signs of physical production. Maybe I just find stuff like that more approachable? Perhaps it's easier to imagine myself one day being capable of making images like that when the working is more obvious... Or perhaps there's just a little bit of a thrill when the magician lets you see behind the curtain a little bit.<br><br>
Anyway, <i>Sōbōtei Must Be Destroyed</i>. It's not just retro visually, but also writing wise. That's not necessarily a good thing, though... the story is repetitive as hell, and circles the drain constantly in a manner typical for old-school horror manga. Constant repititive soliloquies about how frightening the events and surrounds are, page spread after page spread of explosions and action poses. Stuff like that. It gets pretty tiresome after a while, but eventually it dispenses with that stuff and starts focussing more on the relationships between the characters, thank god. As uncompelling as the setting is as a vehicle for horror, some of the dynamics between the characters are pretty terrific. Obviously Deido (the lord of the haunted manor) is totally entertaining. There's a balance struck between aloofness and petulant fury that makes him totally unpredictable, and way more interesting than pretty much any of the other characters, whose motivations are, for the most part, simple as hell. I think the manga's worth reading just for the dynamics between Deido, Takoha and Kurenai, although to avoid spoiling it I'll avoid saying why. Seiichi is also really fun. I'm a total sucker for stunted characters like that. They're vulnerable in a really entertaining way. <br>
Also, I thought it was really fun how the manga primes you to consider all the side characters expendable, and then keeps them around the whole time. A lot of horror and action manga tend to introduce characters and then kill them off immediately, such that the author is forced to either accept a rapidly diminishing cast of characters, or re-introduce the same character over and over again in different guises. For aparticularly egregious example of this, see <i>Riki-Oh</i> (1987-1990), which introduces a new spectator character whose job it is to narrate how badass the protagonist is pretty much every time the guy finds himself in a new place...<br>
I found myself surprised by how elegantly the author of <i>Sōbōtei Must Be Destroyed</i> avoided that. <br>
The last thing I'll say is I hate the way Rokurō looks. That button nose on him doesn't work even a little. He is by far the ugliest character in the manga, and I hated looking at him every single time. <br>
Overall, I enjoyed reading <i>Sōbōtei Must Be Destroyed</i>, but I think if I hadn't gotten to the final arc, I would have hated it. The final confrontation between Takoha and Deido is entertaining enough that it makes a lot of the preceeding drudgery retroactively tolerable.
